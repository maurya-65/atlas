# What each pass checks

Skip what does not apply (a bank has no inventory, a pre-revenue biotech has no margins) and say that you skipped it. Spend the effort where the evidence is.

## Where to look

| Source | What it is good for |
|---|---|
| 10-K (annual report) | Item 1A Risk Factors (compare with last year: which risks were added or dropped), Item 3 Legal Proceedings, Item 7 management's discussion, and the notes on segments, debt and commitments |
| 10-Q (quarterly report) | The same, for the latest quarter, plus "subsequent events" |
| 8-K (current report) | Item 2.02 results; 5.02 director or executive changes; 1.01 big new agreements; 2.05 layoffs and closures; 1.05 cybersecurity incidents; 4.01 auditor change; 4.02 financials that must be restated (serious); 3.01 delisting notice |
| Form 4 | Insider buying and selling. Sales under a pre-arranged "10b5-1" plan are routine; several executives buying with their own money is a real signal |
| Schedule 13D / 13G | Who owns large stakes and whether that is changing |
| DEF 14A (proxy) | How executives are paid, related-party deals, who controls the vote |
| 20-F / 6-K | The equivalents for foreign companies listed in the US |
| Earnings call transcript | Guidance changes, what analysts pressed on, questions the company avoided |
| Regulators and courts | By industry: SEC, FTC, DOJ Antitrust, FDA (approval dates, ClinicalTrials.gov), Commerce/BIS (export controls), USTR (tariffs), NHTSA (cars), FAA (aircraft), CFPB, EU Commission, state attorneys general. Court dockets (CourtListener) for named cases |
| Rivals' own filings | The same period for two or three competitors, to compare |

A full 10-K or 10-Q is often too long to open in one go and gets cut off before the legal and risk notes. Open the filing index or its "R" pages (the statement and note pages), or search for the specific note you need, and say which part you could not read.

Prefer sec.gov and the company's investor-relations page. Use reputable news to explain what the filings show. Do not rest a claim on forums, anonymous blogs or auto-generated stock-news sites. If the only source for a claim is weak, say it is weak.

## 1. Business & market

Compute and show:
- Revenue for the last 6-8 quarters with growth against the same quarter a year earlier, and whether growth is speeding up or slowing. Which segment drives it.
- Gross and operating margin trend. The gap between the company's "adjusted" profit and the official (GAAP) profit, and what is added back. If the gap is large, explain it.
- Operating cash flow, capital spending and free cash flow, and how free cash flow compares with reported profit (persistently lower is a quality warning).
- Cash against debt, debt coming due in the next 24 months, and whether profit covers the interest.
- Share count change over a year: buybacks against shares issued as pay.
- The company's guidance against what happened, for the last four quarters, and what it says about the next one.
- Customers above 10% of revenue (these are disclosed), backlog, and deferred revenue.

Valuation, in the same pass:
- Price against earnings (trailing and forward), enterprise value against sales or EBITDA, and free-cash-flow yield. Compare with the stock's own five-year range and with two or three close peers.
- One sentence on what growth the price assumes ("at 30 times earnings the market is paying for roughly X years of Y% growth"), and what happens to that if growth halves.
- No price target. If a metric means nothing (negative earnings), say so rather than quoting it.
- Put the multiple in years: "200 times earnings means paying for about 200 years of this year's profit".

Red flags: receivables or inventory growing much faster than revenue; margins improving only through cuts; guidance withdrawn or widened; growing add-backs; a restatement or a going-concern note.

## 2. Regulation & legal

For each case, rule or investigation: who is involved, what is claimed, the court or agency, the stage, the next date, and the amount at stake as a share of annual revenue and of cash. Use the company's own disclosure (Legal Proceedings and the contingencies note) and compare it with what the other side says.

- Keep apart: an allegation, a ruling, a settlement, and a fine that has been paid.
- Rules in motion: proposed or final, the effective date, who is affected, and the company's own statement of its exposure.
- Trade: tariffs, export controls and sanctions, and the share of revenue or supply that sits in the affected countries (from the segment and geography notes).
- Antitrust, privacy, product safety and recalls.

## 3. Industry & competition

- Market size and growth claims: whose number is it, the company's or an independent one? Treat "total addressable market" claims sceptically.
- Share of the company and its rivals over time, from the rivals' own results for the same period.
- Pricing power: recent discounting or price cuts, how easily customers can switch.
- Technology shifts that could weaken the company's advantage, and customers who build the product themselves.
- Supply: single suppliers, capacity limits.
- Where the industry is in its cycle (stock clearing, spending cycles, demand drivers).

## 4. People & operations

- Management: tenure, recent departures (8-K 5.02), a change of finance chief (an accounting signal), succession, dependence on one person, how independent the board is.
- Insiders: net buying or selling over 6-12 months, ignoring routine plan sales.
- Workforce: layoffs (8-K 2.05, WARN notices), strikes and union contracts with their expiry dates.
- Operations: plant closures, recalls, outages, safety incidents, cybersecurity incidents, supply-chain trouble.
- Governance: related-party deals, shares with extra votes, pay that does not follow performance.

## 5. Skeptic

Build the strongest honest case against the verdict the other passes are heading for.
- Find the claim with the weakest evidence in the other passes.
- Ask "what would have to be true for this to be wrong?" and test those things.
- Imagine the stock is down 30% in a year: what is the most likely reason?
- Check accounting quality (the red flags above) and who gains from the story being told (company press releases, sellers of the stock).
- Use base rates: how do companies in this situation usually fare?

The Skeptic is not contrary for show. If the case against is weak after a real search, say so, and let that raise confidence.

## If your time or tool budget is short

Do these first and say what you skipped.
- Business & market: the latest two quarters from the company's own results release, free cash flow, cash against debt, and guidance. One valuation multiple against the stock's own history if you can find it.
- Regulation & legal: the legal-proceedings note, plus news from the last 90 days about regulators and court dates.
- Industry & competition: one rival's latest result for the same period.
- People & operations: executive changes and layoffs in the last year from 8-K items 5.02 and 2.05, and net insider selling.
- Skeptic: the single claim that matters most to the verdict, tested.

## Cross-examination

Each pass names the weakest claim in one other pass (the pass, the claim, why it is weak, what evidence would settle it), then looks for that evidence. Record the objection, what was found, and whether the stance moved. Disagreements that remain go into the report.
